# LUMIN

## About
LUMIN is a single-player exploration game about bringing light back to a broken world. You'll collect and shape light to solve puzzles, heal damaged landscapes, and piece together a story told entirely through visuals and atmosphere—no dialogue needed.

## Inspirations & reference titles
- Journey — https://thatgamecompany.com/journey/  
    We love how it tells a moving story without words and gives you that quiet sense of purpose as you move through the world.
- Limbo & Inside (Playdead) — https://playdead.com/games/  
    The way these games build tension and tell stories through their environments (without relying on combat) is exactly what we're aiming for.
- Gris — Nomada Studio  
    This game nails the emotional journey through color and never punishes you for exploring—just lets you experience it.
- Sky: Children of the Light — https://thatgamecompany.com/sky/  
    Their light mechanics are beautiful, though we're taking a more intimate, single-player approach.

## Design goals
- Give players a meaningful reason to explore—something like restoring ancient beacons to bring back lost memories.
- Focus on atmosphere and emotional impact rather than combat or high scores.
- Make exploration feel rewarding without punishing mistakes. We want players to feel curious, not stressed.
- Strike a balance between moments of tension and genuine wonder.

## Core mechanics
- Collect and use light to restore fixtures, trigger memories, and open new paths.
- Light evolves as you progress—becoming brighter, reaching farther, and unlocking special abilities.
- Solve environmental puzzles using different light properties (timed charges, color-matching, reflection).
- Movement feels deliberate and weighty—making each jump and climb matter.

## How LUMIN learns from similar games
- From Journey: Keep the emotional storytelling but give players a clearer sense of what they're doing early on.
- From Limbo/Inside: Use the environment to tell stories and create tension, but explore a wider emotional range beyond just dread.
- From Gris: No fail states and progress that feels healing, but make light interaction more central to gameplay.
- From Sky: Borrow the light-as-resource idea but build a focused single-player experience instead of social features.

## Target audience
This game is for people who appreciate thoughtful, story-driven experiences—the kind of player who values mood and meaning as much as (or more than) action and challenge.

## Quick roadmap ideas
- Build a prototype of the core light mechanic with one puzzle type (like charging distant objects).
- Create three distinct areas, each with its own mood and unique light interactions.
- Design progression that never punishes exploration and gives players an early hook to keep them invested.
- Refine how movement feels—making it weighty enough to create connection and vulnerability.
